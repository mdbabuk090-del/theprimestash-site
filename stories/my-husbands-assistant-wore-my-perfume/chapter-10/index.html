Chapter 10 — A Different Scent

Chapter 10 of 10

Grant’s resignation became public on a Monday.

The statement used words like transition and governance.

It did not mention perfume.

It did not mention Sloane.

It did not mention the voice message telling her to keep transfers under fifty thousand dollars.

Companies preferred clean sentences.

Elise understood that better than anyone.

The internal investigation continued.

Meridian’s remaining assets were frozen.

The bank released Elise from the personal guarantee after the company refinanced the facility under new controls.

Outside counsel referred the relevant financial records for further review.

What happened to Grant after that would take time.

Elise stopped measuring her life by it.

Three months later, she met Teresa for lunch.

Not at the office.

That was new.

Teresa ordered soup and complained that the restaurant’s chairs were too low.

Elise said nothing about the fact that Teresa had chosen the restaurant.

“How is Sloane?” Teresa asked.

“Still cooperating.”

“Coming back?”

“That’s not decided.”

“You trust her?”

“No.”

Teresa lifted an eyebrow.

Elise added, “Trust isn’t the only reason to treat someone fairly.”

Teresa considered that.

Then nodded.

It was as close to agreement as she usually came.

Grant had sent two letters.

Elise returned both unopened through counsel.

The divorce petition was filed.

No dramatic confrontation.

No destroyed house.

No screaming in a parking lot.

Just disclosures.

Schedules.

Signatures.

Asset lists.

The ordinary machinery required to end something people once thought would last forever.

That evening, Elise came home to a quiet house.

She had moved Grant’s remaining things into storage.

The bathroom counter was empty except for her toothbrush and a small package that had arrived that afternoon.

She carried it to the kitchen.

Inside was a perfume sample set.

Six tiny glass vials.

No engraving.

No history.

Elise laughed when she saw them.

For fifteen years, she had ordered the same perfume twice a year.

At first because Grant loved it.

Later because she did.

Eventually because changing it felt unnecessary.

She opened the first vial.

Too sweet.

The second smelled like pine.

The third reminded her of a hotel lobby.

The fourth was warm and dry, with something bright underneath.

Elise put a drop on her wrist.

Then she waited.

Perfume changed after a few minutes.

That was the point.

You were not supposed to judge it the second it touched skin.

She washed the dinner dishes.

Answered two emails.

Watered a plant Grant had always forgotten existed.

Then she smelled her wrist again.

Different now.

Better.

Her phone buzzed.

A message from Teresa.

Board approved the new wire controls. No single-person vendor setup. You won.

Elise read it.

Then typed back.

Nobody won. We fixed the controls.

Three dots appeared.

Same thing.

Elise smiled.

Some people never changed.

She returned to the sink.

The old perfume bottle still sat in the cabinet above it.

She had never poured it out.

Not yet.

Now she took it down.

The liquid caught the kitchen light.

Fifteen years inside a piece of glass.

She removed the cap.

Then tipped the bottle.

The perfume disappeared into the drain.

No ceremony.

No anger.

Just water running afterward until the scent faded.

Elise rinsed the bottle and set it in the recycling bin.

Then she picked up the fourth sample again.

One drop on each wrist.

No engraved name.

No second bottle hidden somewhere else.

Nothing to prove.

She turned off the kitchen light.

As she walked upstairs, the new scent followed her quietly.
