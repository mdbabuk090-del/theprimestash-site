Chapter 09 — The Last Account

Chapter 9 of 10

The total was $1.08 million.

Grant had opened a second investment account nine months earlier.

Before Sloane.

Before the perfume.

Before the affair, as far as Elise could prove.

That mattered.

Not legally.

Emotionally.

For six months, Elise had built a story in which one betrayal led to another.

Grant met Sloane.

Grant became reckless.

Grant moved money.

Grant tried to shift the loss.

The second account destroyed that order.

The money came first.

Sloane had not changed him.

She had entered a structure already built.

Elise sat in Teresa’s office the next morning while outside counsel summarized the findings.

When the call ended, Teresa closed her notebook.

“You look relieved.”

“I am.”

“That is an odd response to another half-million-dollar loss.”

“It means I can stop wondering whether I caused anything.”

Teresa studied her.

“You were wondering?”

“For about five minutes.”

“Five minutes too many.”

Elise smiled.

Teresa did not.

“My son is asking for a meeting.”

“No.”

“He says he’ll cooperate if you attend.”

“He can cooperate without me.”

“He wants to apologize.”

Elise looked at the city through the window.

For years, she had thought closure required a conversation.

A confession.

A sentence that explained why.

Now she had spreadsheets.

They were less poetic and more accurate.

“No,” she said again.

Teresa nodded once.

That afternoon, Sloane signed the cooperation agreement after speaking with her lawyer.

She turned over the voice messages.

Emails.

Photographs.

A handwritten list Grant had given her of transfer amounts to keep below $50,000.

Outside counsel placed her on paid administrative leave while the investigation continued.

No reward.

No public redemption.

No instant promotion.

Just a chance to tell the truth before someone else wrote it for her.

At five, Elise returned home.

She opened the bathroom drawer.

Grant’s ring was still there.

She placed it in a small envelope.

Then she removed her own.

The skin beneath it looked pale.

Fifteen years.

She expected her hand to feel lighter.

It felt exactly the same.

Her phone rang.

Grant.

She let it stop.

He called again.

Then a third time.

Finally, a voicemail appeared.

Elise pressed play.

“Elise.”

His voice sounded tired.

“I know you don’t want to talk to me.”

A pause.

“I know what I did.”

Another pause.

“You’re going to think this is about the money. It isn’t.”

Elise almost deleted it.

Then he said, “I need you to know I never planned to leave you.”

She stared at the wall.

Of all possible apologies, he had chosen that one.

As if staying while lying were a gift.

As if betrayal became smaller because abandonment had not been scheduled.

The message continued.

“Sloane was a mistake. Meridian was supposed to work. I thought I could put everything back before you ever knew.”

There it was.

Not remorse.

Restoration.

He wanted credit for planning to erase the evidence.

Elise deleted the voicemail.

Then she opened the cream perfume box.

One compartment held the half-used bottle.

The other was empty.

Tomorrow, counsel would collect the engraved bottle from her office.

Evidence.

After that, it would belong to a file.

Not to her.

Elise lifted her own bottle.

She carried it to the kitchen.

Unscrewed the top.

Then stopped.

The scent rose between her hands.

Familiar.

Beautiful.

Ruined.

She held it over the sink.

Her phone lit again.

This time the message was from Teresa.

Grant resigned. Effective tonight.
