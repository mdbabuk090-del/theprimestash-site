Chapter 06 — The Sample Problem

Chapter 6 of 10

They went to the lab the next morning.

Peter barely spoke in the car.

Maren did not force him.

Dr. Okoro met them in a small consultation room with no medical posters on the walls.

Only a table.

Three chairs.

A sealed white envelope.

The same envelope was still locked at home.

This was a duplicate prepared from the verified report.

Dr. Okoro placed a chart between them.

“I want to be precise,” she said. “DNA can establish biological consistency. It cannot tell you who raised whom, who loved whom, or who has a legal parental relationship.”

Peter nodded once.

His eyes were red.

“The father-daughter relationship is strongly supported.”

He looked at Maren.

She did not smile.

There was no victory in proving something he should never have doubted.

Dr. Okoro continued.

“The sample labeled Camille Sutcliffe does not show a parent-child relationship with Peter Sutcliffe.”

“How certain?” Peter asked.

“Enough that I would not describe it as an ambiguous result.”

“So the sample is wrong.”

“That is why I requested recollection.”

“She refuses.”

Dr. Okoro folded her hands.

“Then I need to explain the chain issue.”

Maren leaned forward.

“The order form declared a three-generation biological line: grandmother, father, daughter. But the kit assigned to Camille was registered through an archived family account created years ago.”

Peter frowned.

“What archived account?”

“Your mother had prior genetic screening at this lab network in 1998.”

Peter stared.

Dr. Okoro slid over a page.

“We compared the current sample to her archived profile. They are the same person.”

Maren felt the room go cold.

No mislabeled swab.

No accidental switch.

Camille was Camille.

And Camille was not Peter’s biological mother.

Peter looked at the ceiling.

“How did she think this wouldn’t show up?”

Dr. Okoro hesitated.

“I can’t answer motive.”

Maren could.

Camille expected the panel to become a weapon against Maren before anyone read the entire report.

If the daughter failed the paternity portion, Peter would stop caring about the rest.

If she passed, Camille probably intended to intercept the envelope.

Give me the key.

Maren remembered the demand.

Not request.

Demand.

Peter pressed his fingertips into his eyes.

“Can we have the report?”

Dr. Okoro placed one hand on the envelope.

“Yes. But there is another detail.”

Maren’s stomach tightened.

“What?”

“The order included a relationship note submitted by the requesting party.”

“Peter?” Maren asked.

Dr. Okoro looked at him.

“The portal user was Peter Sutcliffe. But the note says, ‘Primary concern is maternal-line continuity for trust purposes.’”

Peter stared.

“I didn’t write that.”

“Do you share access to the account?”

“My mother set it up.”

Maren closed her eyes.

There it was.

Camille had not merely suggested the test.

She had shaped the order.

Dr. Okoro handed Peter the sealed report.

He did not open it.

He turned it over once in his hands.

White paper.

Blue laboratory seal.

The object that had sat in their home for days without being touched.

Maren thought about how much power they had given it.

Peter finally broke the seal.

He read the first page.

Then the second.

His hands began to shake.

Maren touched his wrist.

He looked at her.

“I’m her son.”

“Yes.”

“No matter what this says.”

“Yes.”

His face crumpled.

For the first time since the lab called, Maren reached for him.

Peter leaned into her.

Then his phone rang.

Camille.

He looked at the name.

Did not answer.

A voicemail arrived.

Peter pressed play.

Camille’s voice filled the room.

“I need you to understand why I did it.”

Peter stopped the message.

He looked at Maren.

“She knew this report would exist.”

Maren nodded.

“And she still ordered it.”

“Yes.”

Peter stared at the sealed edge he had just torn open.

“Then she wasn’t trying to protect me.”
