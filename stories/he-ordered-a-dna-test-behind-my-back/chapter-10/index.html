Chapter 10 — The Whole Answer

Chapter 10 of 10

Camille did not come Sunday.

She did not call Monday either.

On Tuesday, she sent Peter a message.

I love you. I don’t know how to make this smaller.

He did not reply.

Not yet.

Maren did not push him.

The trust review ended three weeks later.

Peter was not an eligible biological descendant under the document’s language.

The assets moved to the next branch.

The cousin who used summer as a verb received them.

Peter read the notice once.

Then put it in a drawer.

That night, he ordered pizza.

Maren stared at him.

“What?”

“That’s your reaction?”

“To what?”

“Losing an inheritance.”

He opened a soda.

“I’ve had worse months.”

She laughed.

He did too.

They were not fixed.

That mattered.

The DNA test did not erase what Peter had done.

He had doubted Maren.

He had used their daughter’s trust without telling her the real purpose of the sample.

He had let Camille’s fear become action.

So he started therapy.

He opened every family-office document he had never read.

He changed the passwords on accounts Camille had created for him.

Small things.

Adult things.

Things he should have done years ago.

Maren watched without rewarding him for basic responsibility.

That mattered too.

Camille stayed quiet for almost a month.

Then a package arrived.

No note.

Inside was a photograph.

Peter at six weeks old.

Camille asleep in a hospital chair beside his crib.

Her hand rested through the rails.

On the back, written in faded ink:

My son came home today.

Peter stared at the sentence for a long time.

Maren did not say anything.

Finally he sat down.

“She knew.”

“Yes.”

“And she still wrote that.”

“Yes.”

His eyes filled.

“I don’t know what to do with her.”

“You don’t have to know yet.”

He nodded.

The lab envelope remained in the kitchen drawer.

Maren had considered throwing it away.

Peter said no.

Not because it was proof of blood.

Because it was proof of what happened when nobody asked questions.

Their daughter came home from campus for dinner.

She hugged Peter longer than usual.

Then she asked for dessert before finishing the main course.

Normal.

Messy.

Alive.

Halfway through cheesecake, her phone buzzed.

She looked at the screen.

“Grandma.”

Peter stopped eating.

Maren waited.

Their daughter let the call ring out.

Then she looked at both of them.

“Am I supposed to be mad at her?”

Peter answered first.

“No.”

“Are you?”

“I am a lot of things.”

“That’s not helpful.”

“I know.”

She looked at Maren.

“Why did she stop calling me?”

The question was different this time.

Not asking what had happened.

Asking what it meant.

Maren folded her napkin.

“Because she spent a long time believing family was something you could prove on paper.”

Their daughter frowned.

“But the paper said she wasn’t his mom.”

“No,” Peter said softly. “The paper said she wasn’t my biological mother.”

The daughter looked between them.

“Same thing?”

Peter shook his head.

“No.”

Silence.

Then her phone buzzed again.

Camille.

The daughter stared at it.

“Should I answer?”

Peter looked at Maren.

Maren looked back.

This was not their decision.

Their daughter picked up.

“Hi, Grandma.”

A pause.

Her face softened.

“No, I’m not mad.”

Another pause.

Then she smiled slightly.

“Yes. Dad’s here.”

She held the phone toward Peter.

He stared at it.

Maren thought he would refuse.

Then he took it.

“Hi, Mom.”

Nothing happened.

No one cried.

No one forgave thirty-six years.

Peter just listened.

Across the table, their daughter looked at Maren.

“Was it really all because of the test?”

Maren thought about the sealed envelope.

The trust.

The lies.

The love that had existed beside them.

“No,” she said.

“Then what was it?”

Maren watched Peter listen to his mother.

“Because we finally told the truth.”

Their daughter nodded.

That was the whole answer.
