Chapter 07 — Not Her Son

Chapter 7 of 10

Camille was waiting when they arrived.

She stood in the front room beside a silver tea service no one used.

Maren had seen that room dozens of times.

Family portraits on one wall.

Peter at five.

Peter at twelve.

Peter graduating.

Peter marrying Maren.

Blood everywhere, according to Camille.

Except now.

Peter placed the lab envelope on the coffee table.

Camille looked at it once.

Then at him.

“I can explain.”

“Start with 1990.”

She sat.

Peter remained standing.

Maren stayed near the doorway.

This was not her conversation.

Not yet.

Camille folded her hands.

“You were six weeks old.”

Peter’s face changed.

“What happened?”

“There was a blood test.”

“Why?”

“You were sick.”

Camille looked at the floor.

“The doctor said the blood types didn’t make sense. Your father ordered more testing.”

Peter whispered, “And?”

“You weren’t ours.”

Silence.

Maren felt the words hit the room physically.

Not mine.

Not hers.

Not theirs.

Peter swallowed.

“Hospital switch?”

Camille nodded.

“We were told there had likely been an infant identification error.”

“What happened to your biological child?”

Camille closed her eyes.

“We never found him.”

Peter flinched at the pronoun.

Him.

Another son.

Somewhere.

Or nowhere.

“You knew my whole life?”

“Yes.”

“Dad knew?”

“Yes.”

“Who else?”

“Two lawyers.”

Peter laughed once.

It sounded painful.

“And the trust?”

Camille’s jaw tightened.

“Your grandfather’s document was old-fashioned.”

“Blood descendant.”

“He would have changed it if he knew.”

“Did he know?”

“No.”

“So you lied.”

“I protected you.”

Peter’s eyes filled.

“For thirty-six years?”

“I raised you.”

“You keep saying that like I’m arguing.”

Camille stood.

“You are my son.”

“Yes.”

Maren saw Peter’s shoulders shake.

“That’s why this is worse.”

Camille went still.

Peter pointed at the envelope.

“You told me to test my daughter.”

“I needed certainty.”

“No. You needed a problem below me.”

Camille looked at Maren.

There it was.

The first open admission without words.

Maren stepped forward.

“You thought if the test created doubt about our daughter, Peter would stop before anyone looked at your sample.”

Camille’s face hardened.

“I thought the trust was about to be challenged.”

“By whom?”

“The next branch.”

“The cousin?”

Camille did not answer.

Peter stared at her.

“You were trying to break my marriage to protect an inheritance.”

“I was trying to protect your future.”

“You made me doubt my wife.”

“For a week.”

Maren felt something cold move through her.

Camille had counted the damage in days.

As if duration changed the act.

Peter picked up the envelope.

“What happens now?”

Camille’s eyes went to the report.

“If this reaches the trustees, you lose the distribution.”

“Then I lose it.”

Her face changed.

“No.”

Peter looked almost calm.

“Yes.”

“You don’t understand what you’re saying.”

“I understand for the first time.”

“That money is your life.”

“No. You made it my life.”

Camille stepped toward him.

“Peter.”

He moved back.

That hurt her more than any accusation.

Maren saw it.

Camille’s voice dropped.

“I did everything for you.”

Peter nodded.

“I know.”

For a moment, Maren thought he might forgive her.

Then he said, “That’s why I’m going to tell them myself.”

Camille stared.

“The trustees?”

“Yes.”

“You can’t.”

“I can.”

“You will lose everything.”

Peter looked around the room.

At the portraits.

The silver.

The furniture chosen to look inherited even when it wasn’t.

Then he looked at his mother.

“No,” he said quietly. “I think I already lost the thing you were trying to save.”
