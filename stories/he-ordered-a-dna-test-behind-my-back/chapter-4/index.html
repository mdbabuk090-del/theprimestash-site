Chapter 04 — The Sealed Envelope

Chapter 4 of 10

Maren did not give Camille the key.

Camille asked twice.

The second time, Peter told her to stop.

That surprised everyone.

Camille left without the almond cake.

Maren threw it away.

The envelope stayed locked in the kitchen cabinet for three days.

Each morning, Maren walked past it.

Each night, she checked the seal through the glass panel.

Still intact.

Peter slept in the guest room.

Their daughter called twice from campus.

Peter had told her the truth about the test.

Not all of it.

Enough.

She was furious.

Mostly at him.

A little at Maren for not knowing.

Maren understood.

Children wanted parents to know everything before it could hurt them.

At nineteen, apparently, that expectation survived.

On the third evening, Peter sat across from Maren at the kitchen table.

“I talked to Mom.”

“I’m sorry.”

He almost smiled.

“She says you’re overreacting.”

“That sounds more like her.”

“She also says there’s a trust meeting Sunday.”

“Why?”

“Grandfather’s estate.”

Maren knew the outline.

Peter’s grandfather had built the family’s manufacturing company.

Most of the operating assets had been sold years ago, but a trust still held investment property and voting shares in a private fund.

Peter was due to receive control of his portion at thirty-seven.

His birthday was in six weeks.

“What does the trust say?” Maren asked.

Peter rubbed his thumb against the edge of the table.

“I’ve never read the full document.”

She stared at him.

“It’s your inheritance.”

“I know.”

“You’re about to receive it.”

“I know.”

“And you’ve never read it.”

“My mother handles the family office.”

Maren leaned back.

There it was again.

Peter letting Camille stand between him and paper.

“Get it.”

“What?”

“The trust.”

“Now?”

“Yes.”

Peter called Camille.

No answer.

He called the family office.

No answer.

He sent an email.

At 9:42 p.m., a PDF arrived.

Four hundred twelve pages.

Maren opened it.

Peter groaned.

“Tonight?”

“Yes.”

They read in silence.

The document was dense.

Definitions.

Subtrusts.

Successor trustees.

Distribution provisions.

At page 287, Maren stopped.

“Peter.”

He looked up.

She turned the laptop.

A clause defined eligible descendants.

Biological issue of Harold Sutcliffe, by lineal descent.

Peter read it twice.

“So?”

“So adopted descendants are excluded.”

“I’m not adopted.”

“I didn’t say you were.”

He looked at the locked cabinet.

Neither of them spoke.

Maren scrolled.

If no eligible biological descendant existed in Peter’s branch at the distribution date, the assets passed to the next bloodline branch.

A cousin.

Someone Peter barely saw.

Peter stood.

“This is ridiculous.”

“Why?”

“Because Mom wouldn’t risk this.”

Maren looked at him.

“Risk what?”

He stopped.

Exactly.

They still did not know.

Her phone rang.

Dr. Okoro.

Maren answered immediately.

“I’ve verified the sample records,” the doctor said.

Maren sat straighter.

“And?”

“I need you and your husband to come to the lab.”

“Why?”

“Because one result is straightforward.”

Peter moved closer.

Maren put the call on speaker.

Dr. Okoro continued.

“Your daughter is biologically consistent with Peter Sutcliffe as her father.”

Peter closed his eyes.

Relief crossed his face.

Maren felt something break anyway.

Then Dr. Okoro said, “But that is not the chain issue I called about.”

Peter opened his eyes.

“What is?”

A pause.

“The third sample.”

Camille.

Dr. Okoro’s voice lowered.

“Mrs. Sutcliffe, the reported grandmother relationship is not supported by the genetic data.”
