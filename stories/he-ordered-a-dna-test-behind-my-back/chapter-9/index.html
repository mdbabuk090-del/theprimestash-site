Chapter 09 — The Family Meeting

Chapter 9 of 10

The trust meeting lasted twenty-three minutes.

Maren did not attend.

Peter asked her not to.

He said he needed one thing in his life that was not spoken through someone stronger than him.

Maren respected that.

She waited at a coffee shop across the street.

At 10:17, Peter texted.

Done.

Nothing else.

He arrived fifteen minutes later.

No jacket.

Tie loose.

Lab envelope in his hand.

Maren stood.

“Well?”

“The trustees suspended the distribution.”

She searched his face.

“And?”

“They’re reviewing the bloodline clause.”

“And?”

Peter sat.

Maren waited.

Finally he laughed.

It surprised both of them.

“I thought I’d feel sick.”

“Do you?”

“No.”

He looked down at his hands.

“I feel lighter.”

Maren sat across from him.

“The cousin gets it?”

“Probably.”

“You hate that cousin.”

“I barely know him.”

“You complained about him for ten years.”

“He says ‘summer’ as a verb.”

Maren laughed before she could stop herself.

Peter smiled.

It was the first normal smile in days.

Then it disappeared.

“Mom was there.”

Maren’s laughter faded.

“What happened?”

“She tried to speak before I did.”

“Of course.”

“I stopped her.”

Maren raised an eyebrow.

Peter almost smiled again.

“I said, ‘You’ve had thirty-six years.’”

Maren reached for his hand.

“What did she do?”

“Nothing.”

“Nothing?”

“She sat down.”

That might have been the most shocking part of the entire week.

Peter turned the coffee cup slowly.

“I told them about the hospital error. The lab. The archived sample. The test we ordered for our daughter.”

His voice tightened.

“I told them why we ordered it.”

Maren squeezed his fingers.

“I’m sorry.”

“I know.”

“No. I mean it.”

He looked at her.

“I doubted you because my mother handed me a fear and I treated it like evidence.”

Maren said nothing.

“I want to say I only doubted you for five minutes.”

“Don’t.”

He nodded.

“Right.”

They sat in silence.

Apologies did not repair things instantly.

But a precise apology was at least a tool.

Peter continued.

“The trustees asked whether I wanted to challenge the clause.”

“Do you?”

“No.”

That surprised her.

“There may be legal arguments.”

“I know.”

“You could win.”

“I know.”

“Then why not?”

Peter looked out the window.

“Because I don’t want the next ten years of my life to be about proving I deserve money from a man who wrote me out without knowing me.”

Maren understood.

“What about Camille?”

He looked at the envelope.

“I don’t know yet.”

That answer was healthier than a forced one.

When they got home, their daughter was waiting on video.

Peter answered.

Her face appeared.

“Did you tell them?”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“I probably lost the trust.”

She stared at him.

Then said, “Okay.”

Peter laughed softly.

“Okay?”

“You still have a job.”

“Yes.”

“Mom still has a job.”

“Yes.”

“I’m still biologically yours.”

Peter’s eyes filled.

“Yes.”

She leaned closer to the camera.

“So what exactly did we lose?”

Peter covered his mouth.

Maren looked away.

Their daughter waited.

Then asked, “Is Grandma coming Sunday?”

Nobody answered.

Her expression changed.

“Oh.”

Peter lowered his hand.

“I don’t know.”

The daughter looked at Maren.

“Why has she stopped calling me?”

Maren looked at Peter first.

He nodded.

So Maren answered.

“Because the truth is new,” she said. “And she doesn’t know how to live inside it yet.”
