Chapter 02 — Just to Be Sure

Chapter 2 of 10

Peter put the coffee down.

“Maren, listen.”

“No.”

She stood on the opposite side of the island.

“You listen.”

He rubbed both hands over his face.

“It wasn’t supposed to happen like this.”

“How was it supposed to happen?”

“I was going to tell you.”

“When?”

“After.”

“After what?”

“After the results.”

Maren stared at him.

There were moments in a marriage when the words themselves mattered less than the order.

Peter had planned to test first.

Know first.

Decide first.

Then tell her whatever version of the truth felt safest.

“Why?” she asked.

Peter looked toward the window.

“Mom brought it up.”

Of course.

Maren laughed once.

Not because anything was funny.

“Your mother suggested you test our daughter.”

“It wasn’t like that.”

“What was it like?”

“She said there are things coming up with the trust. Family documentation.”

“DNA is family documentation now?”

“She said it could avoid questions later.”

“Questions from whom?”

Peter’s jaw tightened.

“Lawyers.”

“Which lawyers?”

“I don’t know.”

“You ordered a three-generation DNA test because your mother vaguely mentioned lawyers?”

“She was trying to protect us.”

“From me?”

“No.”

“From our daughter?”

“No.”

“Then from what?”

Peter raised his voice.

“I said I don’t know.”

The kitchen went quiet.

Maren had heard Peter angry before.

Traffic.

Work.

A contractor who missed a deadline.

He almost never raised his voice at her.

That made this worse.

She lowered hers.

“Did you think she wasn’t yours?”

His face changed.

Not enough.

“Yes,” Maren said. “You did.”

“No.”

“You did.”

“For five minutes.”

“That was enough.”

“Maren—”

“You put a swab in our daughter’s mouth.”

“She agreed.”

That stopped her.

“What?”

Peter looked guilty again.

“She thought it was for the family medical history file.”

Maren’s stomach turned.

“She didn’t know?”

“She knew it was DNA.”

“She did not know you were checking whether you were her father.”

“I wasn’t checking only that.”

“Then what else?”

Peter opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Maren understood.

He did not know.

Camille had told him what to order.

He had done it.

Kind in person.

Weak on paper.

That was Peter.

He could spend two hours choosing the exact birthday gift Maren wanted and still sign something enormous because his mother had put a yellow tab on the page.

“Why was Camille tested?”

“Grandparent relationship.”

“Why?”

“It strengthens the panel.”

Maren laughed again.

“You sound like a brochure.”

Peter’s shoulders dropped.

“I’m sorry.”

She looked at him.

“Are you?”

“Yes.”

“Then call our daughter right now and tell her what the test was actually for.”

He went still.

“That would upset her.”

“That is not a reason to lie.”

“I didn’t lie.”

“You let her believe something false because the truth was inconvenient.”

Peter looked down.

Maren picked up her phone.

“No.”

He stepped forward.

“Don’t.”

She stopped.

Not because he asked.

Because she wanted him to feel the space between the request and her answer.

“I’m not calling her,” Maren said. “You are.”

Peter nodded.

His eyes were wet now.

Maren felt no satisfaction.

She had loved him for eighteen years.

Love did not evaporate just because trust cracked.

That was the cruel part.

Her phone buzzed.

A delivery notification.

GENOMIC RELATIONSHIP PANEL — OUT FOR DELIVERY. SIGNATURE REQUIRED.

Peter saw the screen.

His face drained.

Maren turned it toward him.

“The results are coming here.”

He whispered, “Don’t open them.”

She stared.

“That’s exactly what the lab told me.”
