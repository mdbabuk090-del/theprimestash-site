Chapter 05 — The Bloodline Clause

Chapter 5 of 10

Peter did not speak for almost a minute.

Maren watched him stand beside the kitchen table as if the room had moved and he had not.

Finally he said, “The sample was wrong.”

Dr. Okoro was still on speaker.

“That is one possibility we have to exclude.”

“Then exclude it.”

“We are.”

“When?”

“I need a fresh witnessed sample from Camille Sutcliffe.”

Peter looked at Maren.

Maren knew before he said it.

“She won’t do it.”

Dr. Okoro replied, “Then I can only report what the current samples support.”

Peter ended the call.

He paced once around the island.

“My mother is my mother.”

Maren did not answer.

“That test is wrong.”

“It might be.”

“She gave birth to me.”

“That’s what you’ve been told.”

He turned sharply.

“What is that supposed to mean?”

“It means exactly what it means.”

Peter pressed both hands to his head.

“This is insane.”

“Yes.”

“Why would she suggest the test if she knew—”

He stopped.

Maren looked at him.

That was the question.

Why would Camille create a test capable of exposing the one thing she could least afford to expose?

Unless she believed the test would stop somewhere else first.

At Maren.

At their daughter.

Break the marriage.

Create doubt.

Make Peter withdraw from the family panel before anyone examined the upward relationship.

Maren opened the trust document again.

The bloodline clause sat on the screen.

Peter stared at it.

“No.”

“What?”

“No. We are not turning this into inheritance math.”

“Your mother already did.”

“She’s my mother.”

“I know.”

“You keep saying that like it might not be true.”

Maren softened her voice.

“Biologically and actually are not the same question.”

Peter looked at her.

That landed.

For eighteen years, Camille had used blood as a border.

Maren was not born a Sutcliffe.

Their daughter was half outside.

Family meant lineage when it helped Camille.

Now lineage had become a knife pointed inward.

Peter sat.

“What happens if the result is right?”

“The trust may not pass to you.”

“I don’t care.”

Maren believed him.

That surprised her.

Not because Peter was greedy.

He wasn’t.

Because Camille had spent his entire life teaching him that family assets were proof of family belonging.

Losing them would mean losing the thing she had taught him to value.

He opened his phone.

“Call her.”

“You call.”

He did.

Camille answered on the fourth ring.

“Peter.”

“We need another sample.”

Silence.

“The lab says yours didn’t match.”

Another silence.

Then Camille said, “Those tests are not perfect.”

Maren closed her eyes.

Not confusion.

Not shock.

Not even anger.

Camille had skipped directly to defense.

Peter heard it too.

“What do you mean, not perfect?”

“I mean exactly that.”

“Did you know?”

“Know what?”

“That you might not be my biological mother.”

Camille’s breathing changed.

Peter’s face drained.

“Mom.”

“Come over.”

“Answer me.”

“Not on the phone.”

“Answer me.”

Maren had never heard him speak to Camille that way.

For once, Camille did not command the conversation.

“I raised you,” she said.

Peter went still.

That was not an answer.

It was worse.

“When did you know?” he asked.

Camille whispered, “Peter.”

“When?”

A long pause.

“1990.”

Peter was thirty-six.

1990 was the year he was born.

Maren looked at the trust clause.

Then at her husband.

Camille spoke again.

“You need to come here before you decide what this means.”

Peter’s voice broke.

“You already decided what it meant thirty-six years ago.”
