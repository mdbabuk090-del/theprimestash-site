Chapter 08 — I Raised Him

Chapter 8 of 10

Camille called Maren that night.

Peter had turned off his phone.

Maren almost did not answer.

Then she did.

“What?”

Camille was silent for several seconds.

“I need you to stop him.”

“No.”

“You have influence.”

“That is an interesting way to describe marriage after what you did.”

“I made a mistake.”

“You designed a DNA test to damage my marriage.”

“I was under pressure.”

“So was I.”

Camille exhaled sharply.

“You don’t understand this family.”

Maren laughed.

“There it is.”

“What?”

“The sentence you’ve been saying to me for eighteen years without saying it.”

Camille went quiet.

Maren stood at the bedroom window.

Peter was downstairs with their daughter on video call.

Maren could hear his voice.

He had told her everything.

Not just the test.

The hospital switch.

The trust.

The lie.

Their daughter had cried.

Then Peter had cried.

Maren had left them alone.

Camille said, “I raised him.”

“I know.”

“I was there for every fever. Every school meeting. Every broken bone. I slept on the floor next to his bed when he had pneumonia.”

“I know.”

“How can a piece of paper erase that?”

“It can’t.”

Camille stopped.

Maren continued.

“That is the part you still don’t understand.”

“What?”

“The report doesn’t erase that you’re his mother. Your lie is what damaged it.”

Camille’s voice broke.

“I was afraid.”

For the first time, she sounded old.

Not powerful.

Not controlled.

Just afraid.

Maren sat on the edge of the bed.

“Of losing him?”

“At first.”

“And later?”

A long pause.

“Of losing everything built around the lie.”

That was closer.

Camille had made one desperate choice in 1990.

Then another.

Then another.

Eventually the lie had become architecture.

The trust.

The family office.

The way she spoke about blood.

The way she treated Maren as an outsider.

Every rule protected the original secret.

“You could have told him years ago.”

“Yes.”

“Why didn’t you?”

“Because each year made the previous year harder to explain.”

Maren understood that too well.

Small lies did not stay small.

They grew walls.

“What do you want me to do?”

“Ask him to keep the report private.”

“No.”

“He can sign a disclaimer. The trustees never need—”

“No.”

“Maren.”

“No.”

Camille’s voice sharpened.

“You’re enjoying this.”

Maren stood.

“No.”

“You get to watch the family that never accepted you collapse.”

“No.”

“You get to be right.”

Maren looked toward the hallway.

Peter’s voice had stopped downstairs.

She lowered hers.

“I would give anything for this report to say exactly what Peter expected.”

Camille said nothing.

“Because then the only betrayal I would have to deal with is my husband doubting me.”

That landed.

Maren continued.

“You made that betrayal possible.”

“I know.”

“And now Peter has to decide whether thirty-six years of love can survive thirty-six years of silence.”

Camille whispered, “He won’t forgive me.”

“That isn’t mine to decide.”

The call ended.

Maren went downstairs.

Peter sat alone at the kitchen table.

Their daughter had hung up.

The lab envelope lay beside him.

He ran one finger along the torn seal.

“She asked you to stop me.”

“Yes.”

“Will you?”

“No.”

Peter nodded.

He looked relieved.

Then ashamed of being relieved.

“I have the trust meeting tomorrow.”

“I know.”

“What if I can’t say it?”

Maren sat across from him.

“Then start with the first true sentence.”

He looked at her.

“What’s that?”

She thought about it.

Then answered.

“My mother is my mother.”

Peter closed his eyes.

“And the second?”

Maren touched the envelope.

“She is not my biological mother.”

Peter breathed out.

For the first time, the two truths sat beside each other without fighting.
