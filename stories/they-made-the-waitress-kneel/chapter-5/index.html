Chapter 05 — The Report He Wrote

Chapter 5 of 10

Nora read Trent’s termination recommendation twice.

The first page was about Bianca.

Pattern of resistance to management.

Negative influence on team culture.

Unreliable availability.

The second page was about Nora.

Failure to follow instructions.

Hostile questioning of supervisors.

Possible involvement in unauthorized recording.

Nora almost admired the construction.

Every sentence was technically possible.

Together, they were a lie.

Arthur arrived on the fourth floor twenty minutes later.

He looked older when he was angry.

Not weaker.

Sharper.

He placed his cane against Nora’s desk and held out his hand.

“Show me.”

She gave him the report.

He read in silence.

When he finished, he put the pages down carefully.

“Fire him.”

“No.”

“Nora.”

“He thinks this is one bad night.”

“It was enough.”

“For him, maybe.”

She turned her laptop toward Arthur.

Rows of schedules filled the screen.

“Look at the premium shifts.”

Arthur leaned closer.

Nora showed him Bianca’s missing Friday and Saturday hours, then another server, then another.

“Every time someone complains, their best shifts shrink. They either stop complaining or quit.”

Arthur’s face changed.

“And the reports?”

“Voluntary availability.”

He sat down.

For years, Arthur had taught Nora that a company could survive a bad quarter, a bad lease, even a bad expansion.

What it could not survive was learning to lie to itself.

“He made us reward this,” Arthur said.

“Yes.”

The flagship had received two internal performance awards under Trent.

One had been for retention.

Arthur looked toward the floor.

“Then tomorrow cannot just be about a video.”

“That’s why I’m not firing him today.”

At four, Bianca called.

Her voice was quiet.

“I got an email.”

“So did I.”

“Termination review.”

“I know.”

Bianca went silent.

Then, “I can’t lose this job.”

“You haven’t.”

“You don’t know that.”

Nora looked at the building deed open on her second monitor.

She could have ended the fear with one sentence.

Instead she said, “Come tomorrow morning.”

“For what?”

“Your review.”

“You’re suspended too.”

“I’ll be there.”

Bianca gave a nervous laugh.

“You’re weirdly calm about getting fired.”

“I’ve had worse Fridays.”

That night, Trent stayed late.

Nora watched him from the security feed on the fourth floor.

He stood in the empty dining room practicing slides on the bar television.

LABOR DISCIPLINE AS A DRIVER OF SERVICE QUALITY.

STAFFING EFFICIENCY.

ACCOUNTABILITY CULTURE.

He clicked through charts built from the numbers Nora had just proven false.

At nine twenty, he reached the last slide.

FLOOR DISCIPLINE: NEXT STEPS.

Nora zoomed in.

A bullet point read:

Remove resistant employees before behavior spreads.

Under it were two initials.

N.V.

B.R.

Arthur stood behind her.

“He put you in his presentation.”

“He doesn’t know it’s me.”

“No. He thinks you’re disposable.”

Nora closed the security feed.

For the first time that week, she went home before midnight.

At seven the next morning, she dressed in the black server uniform again.

She opened the drawer beside her bed.

The silver leaf pin lay where she had left it after the first night.

Nora picked it up.

Then stopped.

Not yet.

She put it in her coat pocket instead.

At eight forty-five, Bianca waited outside the restaurant.

“You came in uniform,” she said.

“So did you.”

“I figured I should look employable while they fire me.”

Nora smiled.

The elevator doors opened behind them.

Trent stepped out carrying his laptop and the quarterly-review folders.

He saw Nora.

His expression hardened.

“What are you doing here?”

“My termination review.”

“That isn’t until after the board meeting.”

Nora moved aside so he could pass.

“Perfect.”

He frowned.

“Why?”

The private elevator at the end of the lobby chimed.

Three board members stepped out.

Then Arthur Vance.

Trent’s face brightened instantly.

He hurried forward.

“Mr. Vance. I wasn’t told you were coming in person.”

Arthur looked past him.

Straight at Nora.

“Good morning,” he said.

Trent followed his gaze.
