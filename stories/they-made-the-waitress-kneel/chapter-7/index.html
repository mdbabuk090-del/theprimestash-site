They Made the Waitress Kneel. She Owned the Building.

Chapter 07 — The Woman in the Apron

Chapter 7 of 10

Trent looked around the room.

Nobody spoke.

Nora walked to the empty chair beside Arthur.

The silver leaf on her apron caught the light.

Trent stood slowly.

“What is this?”

Arthur sat down.

The board followed.

Nora remained standing.

“Finish your presentation.”

Trent looked at Arthur, then at Nora.

“I’m sorry, I don’t understand.”

“You were explaining why N.V. should be removed before her behavior spreads.”

His eyes dropped to the initials on the screen.

Then back to her.

Something in his face finally broke.

Arthur folded his hands.

“Mr. Halloway, have you met Nora Vance?”

Trent did not answer.

Nora took the remote from the table.

She advanced one slide.

A company ownership chart appeared.

VANCE HOSPITALITY GROUP.

Below it, VANCE HOSPITALITY PROPERTIES.

Below that, the address of the flagship building.

Nora tapped the screen.

“This company owns the restaurant group.”

Another tap.

“This company owns the building.”

Another.

“My name is on both.”

Trent’s eyes went to Arthur.

“Mr. Vance built this company.”

“He did. Four years ago he signed controlling ownership to me and kept the chairman’s seat.”

Arthur did not correct her.

“The board still calls him out of habit,” Nora said. “Habit is not an org chart.”

Trent stared at the screen.

The room seemed smaller now.

Nora set the remote down.

“You told me Friday night that table fourteen was worth more than I was.”

“I did not say that.”

“You asked if I knew what the table was worth.”

“That was about guest retention.”

“You made me kneel.”

“The guest requested—”

“And you agreed.”

Trent swallowed.

Nora turned to the screen again.

The video began without sound.

There she was on the floor.

White towel.

Red wine.

Trent standing over her.

Checking his watch.

Nora paused the frame there.

“This is not why we’re here.”

Trent looked almost relieved.

She advanced to the next slide.

Schedules filled the screen.

“These are the last six months of premium shifts.”

Red marks showed every shift removed after an employee complaint.

Then came the retention reports.

“Those same employees were recorded as voluntarily reducing availability.”

Trent’s mouth opened.

Nora kept going.

“Employees who left after losing shifts were recorded as personal departures.”

Another slide.

“Three complaints from Bianca Reyes.”

Another.

“Zero filed.”

Trent looked at Arthur.

“You’ve already decided.”

“No,” Nora said. “I spent six days deciding.”

His eyes snapped back to her.

“I worked your floor. I closed your dining room. I carried trays, reset tables, got yelled at over steak temperatures, and watched people check your face before they answered simple questions.”

“You came in under false pretenses.”

“I came in under my first name.”

“You concealed who you were.”

“Yes.”

“Then none of this was normal behavior. People acted differently because—”

“They did not know who I was.”

The sentence stopped him.

Nora let the silence sit.

Then she reached into a folder.

She pulled out the termination recommendation.

Two names.

Nora Vance.

Bianca Reyes.

“You wrote this yesterday.”

Trent’s face had gone pale.

“I was following procedure.”

“No. You were creating procedure after the fact.”

Arthur leaned back.

Trent looked toward him again.

“Mr. Vance, I have given twelve years to this company.”

Arthur’s voice was quiet.

“So has the woman you made kneel.”

Trent blinked.

Nora almost corrected him.

She had given nine.

It did not matter.

She closed the folder.

“I’m not firing you today.”

Everyone looked at her.

Trent most of all.

His relief came too fast.

Nora picked up the stained white towel she had brought from the service laundry downstairs.

She placed it on the table in front of him.

“You’re going to apologize to every employee whose shifts you cut after they complained.”

Trent stared at the towel.

“By name,” Nora said. “In person.”

He looked up.

“For how long?”

“Thirty days.”

“And if I refuse?”

Nora met his eyes.

“Then you can explain your resignation before lunch.”
