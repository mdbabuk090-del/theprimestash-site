Chapter 04 — Nothing on Paper

Chapter 4 of 10

By Thursday morning, the video had passed a million views.

Nora knew because Arthur texted her the number.

She did not open the link.

On the restaurant floor, nobody mentioned it.

That silence told her more than comments ever could.

At noon, Trent called Bianca into his office.

The door stayed open.

Nora was polishing glasses ten feet away.

“You’ve become a distraction,” Trent said.

Bianca stood with both hands locked behind her back.

“I came to work.”

“You argued with management.”

“I said Nora’s name.”

“You continued the discussion after I ended it.”

“You cut my shifts.”

“I adjusted staffing.”

Bianca’s chin lifted.

“You punished me.”

Trent smiled.

“Show me where it says that.”

Nora stopped polishing.

There it was again.

Nothing on paper.

Trent pulled a form from his desk.

“This is a final warning.”

Bianca stared at it.

“I’ve never had a first warning.”

“This is not a courtroom.”

“No,” Nora said from the doorway. “It isn’t.”

Trent looked up.

“I didn’t invite you.”

Nora stepped inside.

“Put the warning on me.”

Bianca turned. “Nora—”

“The incident started with me.”

Trent’s face hardened.

“You are both making this much more difficult than it needs to be.”

Nora looked at the form.

“What exactly did Bianca do?”

“I don’t have to explain personnel decisions to another server.”

“Then explain them to her.”

For several seconds, nobody moved.

Trent finally folded the paper in half.

“You know what? Fine.”

He looked at Bianca.

“Go back to the floor.”

Bianca hesitated.

“Now.”

She left.

Nora remained.

Trent shut the door.

“You think she’s your friend?”

“I think she’s an employee.”

“You’ve been here a week.”

“And?”

“And people like Bianca always find someone else to blame. Rent, schedules, managers, customers. There’s always a reason their life isn’t working.”

Nora felt her hand tighten around the polishing cloth.

“Is that what you put in the reports?”

Trent stared at her.

“What reports?”

“The retention reports.”

The room changed.

Only slightly.

But Nora saw it.

Trent recovered quickly.

“Someone’s been talking.”

“Have they?”

He stood.

“You’re suspended for the rest of today.”

“For asking a question?”

“For insubordination.”

“Will that be in writing?”

His mouth flattened.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Nora took off her apron.

She left through the front lobby, not the employee door.

The brass plaque hung beside the entrance.

VANCE HOSPITALITY GROUP.

The old silver leaf sat beneath the lettering.

She stopped in front of it.

A hostess looked at her, confused.

Nora touched the empty spot on her apron where the pin had been.

Then she went upstairs.

The fourth floor belonged to Vance Hospitality Properties, the real-estate company Nora had created when she bought the building eighteen months earlier.

Trent had never been invited up.

He thought the landlord was another Vance subsidiary run by lawyers.

The elevator opened into a quiet office.

Nora opened her laptop.

She pulled Trent’s last four quarterly reports.

Then the original scheduling exports.

The difference appeared within ten minutes.

Friday and Saturday shifts removed from servers after complaints were being counted as voluntary availability reductions.

Employees who quit after losing shifts were marked as personal departures.

Formal complaints dropped only because Trent had stopped filing them.

Nora compared timestamps.

Then she found Bianca.

Three complaints.

Zero recorded.

Two premium shifts removed.

One “availability adjustment.”

Nora sat back.

The video had shown cruelty.

The files showed a system.

Her phone rang.

Arthur.

“The board wants an emergency quarterly meeting tomorrow,” he said.

Nora looked down through the glass wall toward the restaurant floors beneath her.

“Move it to this building.”

Arthur paused.

“Does Trent know who you are yet?”

“No.”

“Are you going to tell him?”

Nora closed the report.

“Not today.”

A new email appeared.

It was from Trent.

Subject: TERMINATION REVIEW.

Attached was a recommendation with two names.

Nora Vance.

Bianca Reyes.
