Chapter 02 — Take It Off

Chapter 2 of 10

“Take it off.”

Trent’s eyes stayed on the silver leaf.

Nora glanced toward table twelve. The woman with the phone had lowered it, but she had not put it away.

“Why?” Nora asked.

“I told you. It isn’t part of the uniform.”

“It’s a pin.”

“It’s unauthorized.”

Nora almost smiled.

Unauthorized was the kind of word Trent liked. Clean enough to put in an email. Empty enough to mean whatever he needed.

She unclasped the silver leaf.

Two tiny holes remained in the black fabric.

Trent held out his hand.

“You want the pin?”

“I want you to stop arguing with me.”

“I wasn’t arguing.”

His jaw tightened.

Nora closed her fingers around the leaf. “I’ll put it away.”

Trent seemed ready to insist. Then table fourteen raised an empty wineglass, and his smile returned before he reached them.

Nora went to the narrow employee hallway and opened her locker.

She placed the pin inside the inner pocket of her handbag.

Her thumb rested on it for a second.

The original Vance company logo had been a leaf hand-drawn by her grandmother on the back of a grocery receipt. The restaurants had changed logos twenty-six years ago.

Only two versions of the old leaf remained.

The pin in Nora’s handbag.

And a brass plaque mounted in the flagship lobby.

She closed the locker.

When she returned, Trent looked at the empty place on her apron.

“Better.”

He tapped his tablet.

“You’re section four for the rest of the night.”

“I thought I was section two.”

“You were.”

Section four had seven tables.

One had already sent a steak back twice.

Nora knew exactly what he was doing.

Nothing written down.

Nothing appealable.

Just enough extra work to make the point.

“All right.”

At nine forty, she had five coffees, a martini, three checks, and a couple arguing about whether they had ordered one dessert or two.

Bianca appeared beside her carrying a bus tub.

“I can take eighteen.”

“No.”

“You’re drowning.”

“I’m fine.”

“You are carrying four coffees and a martini.”

“Five coffees.”

Bianca stared at the tray.

Nora smiled. “Table sixteen changed their mind.”

She delivered everything without spilling a drop.

At ten thirty-two, the front doors closed.

The staff began resetting tables.

Trent called everyone together near the bar.

“Two minutes.”

Nora stood at the back.

Trent folded his arms.

“Tonight we had an avoidable guest-service issue.”

Bianca looked sharply at Nora.

Trent continued.

“When a guest asks for a reasonable correction, we do not debate it on the floor.”

Nora said nothing.

One server stared at the carpet.

Another suddenly became interested in polishing a glass.

Trent’s gaze landed on Bianca.

“And we do not undermine managers in front of guests.”

Bianca blinked. “I said her name.”

“You challenged the chain of command.”

“I said one word.”

“You’re off Friday and Saturday.”

Bianca’s mouth opened.

Those were the best shifts of the week.

“You can’t just—”

“I just did.”

Nora watched Bianca’s face.

Three months behind on rent, according to the retention note Nora had read last week.

Trent’s report had classified it as “resolved scheduling preference.”

Nora stepped forward.

“Put the shifts on me.”

Trent turned.

“This doesn’t involve you.”

“It happened because of me.”

“No. It happened because she forgot who she works for.”

Bianca went still.

Nora felt something cold settle behind her ribs.

“Who does she work for?” Nora asked.

Trent gave her a long look.

Then he smiled.

“Not you.”

He walked away.

Bianca waited until he disappeared into the office.

Then she whispered, “I’m going to get fired.”

Nora looked toward the lobby.

The old brass plaque caught the low light.

VANCE HOSPITALITY GROUP.

Below the words sat the same silver leaf Trent had just ordered her to remove.

Nora’s phone vibrated inside her apron.

A message from her grandfather.

Call me. The video is already online.
