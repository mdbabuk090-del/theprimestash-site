She Pushed His Daughter Into the Pool. Then He Came Home Early.

Chapter 06 — Someone Else Was Recording

Chapter 6 of 9

Adrian sent the message to every guest who had attended the party.

Please do not delete any photos or videos taken at my home that evening. Even if they seem unimportant.

Most responses were useless.

Dinner photos.

Champagne.

A video of the string quartet.

Someone filming the pool lights.

Then, at 11:08 that night, a woman named Caroline Reed called.

“I think I have something.”

Adrian drove to her house himself.

Caroline looked uncomfortable when she handed him her phone.

“I was filming the decorations.”

The video began.

The pool filled most of the frame.

Guests moved through the foreground.

At first, nothing.

Then Adrian saw pale blue.

Lily.

Farther behind her—

Vivian.

His thumb stopped.

“Play it.”

Lily walked toward the pool.

Vivian followed.

The camera shifted slightly as Caroline laughed at something offscreen.

Then Vivian raised both arms.

Both hands landed on Lily’s back.

One sudden movement.

Lily went forward.

Gone.

Caroline’s phone jerked downward at the splash.

The rest became blurred legs, voices and confusion.

The video did not show Lily gripping the edge.

It did not show Vivian’s shoe on her hand.

It did not need to.

Adrian replayed the push.

Once.

Twice.

On the third time, he stopped before Lily hit the water.

He couldn’t watch it again.

Caroline whispered:

“I didn’t realize what I’d recorded.”

“You didn’t do anything wrong.”

“I saw people freeze.”

Adrian looked at her.

“What do you mean?”

“Your wife said something.”

“Was it on the video?”

“No. I had lowered my phone.”

“What did she say?”

Caroline thought.

“I couldn’t hear every word. Something about not helping.”

Another witness.

Adrian sent the original file directly to the detectives handling the incident.

No editing.

No screenshots.

The full recording.

Then he called Nora.

“I need you to give them your statement.”

Silence.

“Nora?”

“What happens to my job?”

“You keep it.”

“My family?”

“They keep theirs.”

“And Mrs. Cole?”

Adrian looked at the paused image on Caroline’s phone.

Vivian’s palms against Lily’s back.

“She doesn’t decide what happens to you anymore.”

A catering server gave a statement the next morning.

He hadn’t seen the initial push.

But he had heard Vivian say:

“I said no one helps her.”

Nora had never spoken to him about the incident.

Two witnesses.

Same sentence.

The deleted security footage suddenly looked less like a technical mystery and more like a cover-up.

But Adrian still didn’t understand one thing.

Why?

Hating Lily wasn’t enough.

Control wasn’t enough.

Not for this.

So he did what he should have done weeks earlier.

He ordered an independent review of the proposed company sale.

Every email.

Every adviser.

Every fee.

Every person who stood to make money if it closed before Lily’s birthday.

Forty-eight hours later, the auditor called.

“Mr. Cole?”

“Yes?”

“We found something involving your wife.”

Adrian’s grip tightened around the phone.

“How much?”

A pause.

“Enough that you should sit down.”
