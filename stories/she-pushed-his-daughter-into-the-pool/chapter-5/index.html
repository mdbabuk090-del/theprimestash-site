Chapter 05 — Ninety-Three Days

Chapter 5 of 9

The call from the family attorney came that afternoon.

“Adrian, there’s something I didn’t think was important at the time.”

“Tell me.”

“It involves Lily’s trust.”

Adrian sat straighter.

“What about it?”

“Vivian contacted me two weeks ago.”

He went still.

“Why?”

“She asked what happens when Lily turns eighteen.”

Adrian glanced toward the calendar on his desk.

Lily’s eighteenth birthday was ninety-three days away.

Her mother’s estate plan was simple.

Until Lily turned eighteen, Adrian acted as trustee over the shares her mother had left her.

On her eighteenth birthday, direct voting control transferred to Lily.

Eighteen percent of Cole Holdings.

Not enough to run the company.

More than enough to matter.

Especially because the proposed sale required a supermajority.

“What exactly did Vivian ask?”

The attorney paused.

“She wanted to know whether voting control could remain with a guardian or trustee if Lily were considered incapable of managing her own affairs.”

Adrian didn’t speak.

“She asked about medical findings. Mental-health evaluations. Legal incapacity.”

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“She framed it as estate planning.”

“And you believed her?”

“So did you.”

The words weren’t cruel.

That made them worse.

Adrian leaned back.

“What did you tell her?”

“That a seventeen-year-old being emotional, difficult or in therapy obviously doesn’t make her legally incapable of managing property.”

“And?”

“She kept asking.”

Adrian thought about the last six months.

Vivian suggesting Lily needed a psychiatrist.

Vivian documenting arguments.

Vivian calling Lily unstable.

Vivian telling friends Lily had “serious behavioral issues.”

Not one event meant much on its own.

Together, they looked different.

“Was she building a record?” Adrian asked.

“I can’t know that.”

But they both knew why he had asked.

Later that evening, Adrian sat beside Lily at the hospital.

“Did you and Vivian ever discuss your shares?”

Lily looked wary.

“Why?”

“Just answer me.”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“A few times.”

“What did she say?”

Lily picked at the edge of her blanket.

“She wanted to know what I’d do when I turned eighteen.”

“And?”

“I told her I wouldn’t vote for the sale.”

Adrian looked at her.

“What sale?”

“The company.”

“Why do you know about that?”

“I heard her on the phone.”

“With who?”

“I don’t know.”

Lily continued.

“She said everything had to be finished before my birthday.”

Adrian’s pulse changed.

“What exactly did you hear?”

“She said, ‘Once Lily controls those shares, she can block it.’”

He stared at her.

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“I did.”

Adrian froze.

Lily looked directly at him.

“You told me I shouldn’t listen to private conversations.”

He remembered.

God.

He remembered.

Three weeks earlier.

Lily had tried to tell him Vivian was talking about the company.

He had been late for a meeting.

He had barely looked up from his phone.

“Did Vivian say anything else?”

Lily hesitated.

“After I told her I wouldn’t sign anything, she smiled.”

“What did she say?”

Lily swallowed.

“She said, ‘You really think your opinion will still matter by then?’”

Adrian felt the room become very quiet.

Ninety-three days.

Vivian didn’t simply dislike Lily.

She needed something to happen before Lily turned eighteen.

And for months—

she had been telling everyone Lily couldn’t be trusted.
