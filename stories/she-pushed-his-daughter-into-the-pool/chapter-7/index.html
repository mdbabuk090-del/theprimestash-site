Chapter 07 — You Weren’t Supposed To Come Home

Chapter 7 of 9

Vivian came home at seven.

Adrian was waiting in the dining room.

No staff.

No guests.

Just two glasses of water and a phone on the table.

“You wanted to talk?”

“Sit.”

Vivian studied him.

Then did.

Adrian slid a folder across the table.

She didn’t touch it.

“What is that?”

“The sale.”

“I don’t work for Cole Holdings.”

“No.”

He looked at her.

“But apparently you planned to profit from selling it.”

For the first time, Vivian’s face tightened.

The independent review had uncovered a side agreement.

If the acquisition closed, a consulting company controlled by Vivian’s brother would receive a substantial success fee.

Emails showed Vivian had helped negotiate it.

One message referred to the money as:

our exit.

Another warned:

The girl becomes a problem after eighteen.

Vivian looked at the documents.

“They’re being taken out of context.”

“Whose context makes that sentence better?”

“Adrian—”

“Did you delete the pool footage?”

“No.”

“Did you ask my attorney how to keep control of Lily’s shares after her eighteenth birthday?”

“I was protecting this family.”

“From my daughter?”

“From a seventeen-year-old making decisions she doesn’t understand.”

Adrian stared at her.

“She understood enough to say no.”

Vivian leaned back.

“And you think that makes her qualified to decide the future of a company?”

“It was her mother’s property.”

There.

A flicker.

The word mother still did something to Vivian.

“She has spent years worshipping a dead woman.”

“She was her mother.”

“And I was expected to live in her shadow.”

Adrian’s expression changed.

“This was about her?”

“It was about everything.”

Vivian’s voice remained controlled, but the polish was cracking.

“The house. The shares. Lily undermining me every time I turned around. You letting a child decide whether we could move forward with our lives.”

“Move forward?”

Adrian took out his phone.

“Is this what you call moving forward?”

He pressed play.

Caroline’s video appeared.

Vivian watched herself walk behind Lily.

Raise her hands.

Push.

The color left her face.

Neither of them spoke.

The video ended.

Adrian waited.

Vivian stared at the black screen.

Then she whispered:

“You weren’t supposed to come home that night.”

Adrian went completely still.

Vivian seemed to hear her own words too late.

“I mean—”

“I know what you mean.”

“No, you don’t.”

“Then explain it.”

“She was going to ruin everything.”

“Lily?”

“She was going to block the sale. She would have made you choose.”

“She’s my daughter.”

“And I’m your wife!”

Adrian’s expression became almost calm.

“That was your mistake.”

Vivian stared at him.

“What?”

“Thinking those two things were equal.”

The doorbell rang.

Vivian looked toward the hall.

Then back at Adrian.

“Who is that?”

He didn’t answer.

Two detectives entered moments later.

Vivian stood.

“Adrian.”

For the first time since he had known her—

she sounded afraid.

One detective stepped forward.

“Mrs. Cole, we need you to come with us.”

Vivian looked at her husband.

“You planned this?”

Adrian’s eyes stayed on hers.

“No.”

He looked toward the phone.

“You did.”
