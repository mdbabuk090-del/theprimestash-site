Chapter 09 — This Time, He Listened

Chapter 9 of 9

Ninety-three days later, Lily Cole turned eighteen.

There was no ballroom.

No orchestra.

No guest list with a hundred names on it.

She only wanted pancakes.

Adrian made them badly.

Nora brought flowers.

At ten that morning, Lily stood in front of the mirror and fastened her mother’s necklace around her neck.

At three in the afternoon, she walked into the boardroom of Cole Holdings.

Adrian walked beside her.

But he did not speak for her.

Not anymore.

Under her mother’s trust, direct voting control of Lily’s shares had transferred to her that morning.

Eighteen percent.

The proposed sale was still on the table.

Without Lily’s support, it could not reach the required voting threshold.

One of the directors began presenting the financial projections.

Lily listened.

Asked two questions.

Then closed the file in front of her.

“My answer is no.”

The room went silent.

The director looked at Adrian.

Adrian didn’t move.

Lily noticed.

A few months ago, she would have expected him to step in.

Correct her.

Explain for her.

Tell everyone what she really meant.

This time, Adrian only said:

“You heard the shareholder.”

The sale failed.

By one vote.

The vote of the very girl Vivian had spent months convincing everyone was too unstable to make decisions for herself.

The independent review continued.

The hidden financial arrangement connected to Vivian’s family was turned over to the board and investigators.

More emails were uncovered.

More questions followed.

Combined with the guest’s video, the witness statements, and the logs showing the deleted security footage, what Vivian had done became harder and harder to deny.

Adrian stopped telling Lily every detail.

Not because he was hiding anything from her.

Because Lily asked him to.

“I don’t want every day of my life to be about her.”

So they made sure it wasn’t.

Lily went back to school.

She started seeing a therapist she had chosen herself.

And eventually—

she went back into the water.

Not the mansion pool.

Not right away.

The first time was at the lake house.

Adrian sat several meters away, pretending to read while Lily stood in the water for almost ten minutes.

At first, only to her ankles.

Then her knees.

Then her waist.

She didn’t go any farther.

And that day—

she didn’t need to.

A few weeks after her birthday, Lily asked Adrian to drive her somewhere.

He knew where before she gave him the address.

Her mother’s grave.

They arrived just before sunset.

Lily didn’t bring flowers.

She had once said her mother hated cut flowers because:

“The moment you cut them, they’ve already started dying.”

Instead, Lily sat down on the grass.

Adrian stayed a few steps behind.

Lily touched the pale-blue stone resting at her throat.

“I got it back, Mom.”

The wind moved softly through the trees.

For a long time, she said nothing else.

Then, quietly:

“I almost didn’t.”

Adrian looked away.

This moment wasn’t his.

A few minutes later, Lily stood.

She brushed the grass from her dress and walked back toward her father.

At the car, Adrian opened the passenger door for her.

“Lily?”

She looked at him.

“I know I can’t undo what happened.”

“I know.”

“And I know saying I’m sorry doesn’t fix it.”

Lily was quiet for a moment.

“No.”

Adrian nodded.

“But I can do better from here.”

Lily studied him.

Three months ago, she might have answered immediately.

Now she took her time.

Then she said:

“Then do that.”

Adrian nodded.

No vow.

No speech.

No grand promise he didn’t yet know how to keep.

Just that.

Lily got into the car.

Before closing the door, she touched the necklace once more.

Vivian had thrown it to the bottom of the pool because she believed some things could simply be made to disappear.

A necklace.

The memory of a dead woman.

A girl’s voice.

She had been wrong about all three.

The necklace had come back.

The truth had come back.

And this time, when Lily spoke—

her father truly listened.

— THE END —
